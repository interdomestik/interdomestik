## How to read a rule

- **Rule.** What to do, in the form "before X, do Y". An implementer needs the **Rule** and **Checked by** paragraphs of the rules that `AGENTS.md` names for the task; the other paragraphs are for review (added after the drill of R3).
- **What binds** (closing pass, review comment R3-C07; reconciled in the delta fix round of 2026-11-18, comment D03 of the delta check). `AGENTS.md` holds the rules, and it decides wherever this file and it differ. This file gives each rule's reasons, its check and its evidence, and its **Rule** paragraph says at length how the line in `AGENTS.md` is read. A **Rule** paragraph binds only where `AGENTS.md` names a passage of it; whatever else it says beyond its line is this review's reading, a guide for the implementer and the reviewer, and no rule of its own. **Six passages of this file bind as they are written, because `AGENTS.md` names them:** (1) **the procedure** in R-01, including the lead definition (named by R-01); (2) **the marker and the pre-check rule** of R-04 (the two sentences in bold in its paragraph "Local verification"; named by the paragraph "Local verification" of section 3); (3) **the API record** of R-04 (the sentence in bold in its paragraph "The API record"; named by tier T4 of section 3; final round of 2026-11-18); (4) **the interim form of R-05** (in its **Rule** paragraph, from "Until WP-02 has extended the wrapper" to the end of the paragraph; named by R-05); (5) **the record of R-37** and (6) **the signs of R-37** (the two lists under its **Rule** paragraph; named by R-37). **`AGENTS.md` also names:** (7) **merging by the lead** in R-01 (named by R-01); (8) **the review bar** and (9) **the skips** of R-02 (named by R-02); (10) **tier narrowing** ("narrowing"), (11) **Mac use**, (12) **instruction-only work**, (13) **the gate flake exception** ("flake") and (14) **no carry-over** of R-04 (named by the paragraph "Local verification" of section 3). Each binds as written. **The first edit of this file after the adoption performs its split** (the owner, 2026-11-18), preserving all six binding passages and their incorporation, the working references and the guard's heading requirements. The earlier wording of this paragraph said that a **Rule** paragraph adds no rule and named two passages, while R-01 and R-04 held the owner's answer 39 without a name in `AGENTS.md`; the delta check named that (comment D03). Two requirements that earlier drafts had left to this file stand in `AGENTS.md` itself: the conflict requirement of R-17, that zero changed rows is a conflict, and the size policy of R-10 (comment D02). R-32 has no line in `AGENTS.md`, by the owner's decision of 2026-11-17 (question 40): the required check `commitlint` states and enforces the convention, and R-32 is kept here as its record.
- **Checked by.** `ENFORCED` names the hook, lint rule, test or CI check. *exists* means it runs today, with its path. *proposed* means it is specified in the register at the end of this file and becomes real only when the owner approves it; until then the rule is advisory in practice. `ADVISORY` says why a machine cannot check it.
- **Evidence.** Finding ids of `shared/findings.json` with their severity. **2P** means both skeptic passes confirmed the finding (CR-COMMON §4.7); the passes for every finding used here are listed in the evidence register. A measurement is cited with its R1 section. An owner decision is cited by its section.
- **Failing example.** A place in the pinned tree, or a merged PR, that the rule would flag.
- **Toward the destination.** The layer of the target architecture the rule serves: front door → proxy → authentication boundary → tenant context → domain packages → PostgreSQL with RLS (`inputs/R2-owner-input.md` §1).
- **Changes.** The files a change for this rule would touch.

Marks: **[S0]**, **[S1]** = the rule answers a finding of that severity; **[class]** = it answers a recurring defect class of the fix sample (A2). Every rule with one of these marks has an enforcement proposal.

## 1. Roles and review

### R-01 — Who implements, who reviews
- **Rule.** Follow the procedure on every change and the additional HIGH steps of R-02. No model approves its own work.
- **Procedure** (owner, 2026-11-28: "I agree on proopsed procedure"). **"The lead" is the Claude Opus session that directs the work for the owner and routes the reviews, never a helper.**
  1. Sol (`gpt-6.2-sol`, `ultra`) writes the code and tests. The tests must be able to fail for the defect they cover.
  2. Sol self-reviews in fresh context and corrects findings until clean. Only independent reviewers give approval verdicts.
  3. Run M5 machine checks on the local head for every change. Check types, unit tests, RLS on a real database, the tenant guard and Sonar preflight. Require M5's `PREPUSH-OK`. Run the other pre-push lanes in R-04 where applicable.
  4. Obtain one Astra YES on the exact local head before any push, subject to R-02's skips. Later changes get an Astra delta review under R-02.
  5. Push and wait for hosted CI green and zero Sonar and CodeQL findings on the change.
  6. The Claude lead reviews the exact head, runs the M4 gate under R-04 on that head and merges with the record below. This timing supersedes the parallel hosted-check timing in R-04's Mac use and No carry-over. R-04 governs gate applicability, evidence and approved gate exceptions. Only `GATE-EVIDENCE` lines count as gate evidence.

The lead may merge only when the PR is up to date with `main` and its exact head has every required check green, including `delivery-gate`, the required YES verdicts with no open finding, and the tier's recorded local verification under R-04. The PR records who merged, the head, each reviewer and model actually served, the tier and the evidence. The owner may merge. No other agent merges. No agent bypasses a ruleset or force-pushes. Releases, deploys, repins, boundary authorizations and staging and production writes stay with the owner (R-24, R-37).
- **What a review is.** Reviews are read-only and report findings by severity with file and line, in the vocabulary of `AGENTS.md`. A `rejected` finding cites repository evidence. A doubt or a blocked reviewer leaves approval pending. Stop and go to the owner after three consecutive NOs. No paid API fallback.

### R-02 — When Astra is used
- **Rule.** Astra (`gpt-6.2-astra`, high effort or above) independently reviews every change under R-01, subject to the skips below. Review the exact local head once before push and review any later change by delta against the previously reviewed head. The delta verdict names the new head.
- **Review bar** (owner, 2026-11-28). Stop and report under R-37 when its boundary or signs apply. The additional HIGH steps apply only to auth, tenancy, database or payments work, and to any R-37 stop. For that work, obtain Sol's design, Astra's early scope approval and the owner's R-37 authorization before writing code. Before push, the Claude lead also gives a focused review of whether the diff follows the design, the tests can fail and the boundary holds. A T6 instruction change outside that scope follows the single pre-push Astra review under R-01. R-04 still governs tiering and verification. Apply the review criteria by threat class. The lead names the class in each brief and decides a reviewer's reasoned objection on the record. Where several classes fit, the higher applies.
  - **A, boundary code**: auth, sessions, tenant, RLS, money, documents, privacy, data integrity; workflows that run with secrets or decide a merge; instructions that decide who reviews or approves or what evidence permits a merge (`AGENTS.md` and the passages it names here); the S2 guard. A blocker is a realistic path to a wrong money, auth, tenant or data outcome for an outside actor, a user or an operator in the deployed configuration, or to untrusted content executing or writing in a trusted run; an edge that needs hostile content already in the trusted repository is hardening or a limit.
  - **B, defect-class checks** (screen contract, mutation checks, public-entry registry, selectors, reports), with advisory tooling, other documents and test-only work. A blocker is only one of R-10's three conditions: it misses a seeded defect of its class; it refuses a good change among the last 20 merged PRs; it passes on missing, empty, unparseable or valid-but-empty input. Untrusted content that can execute or write in a trusted run makes the work class A.
  - **C, out-of-configuration edges**: states that a journal-built database, the shipped configuration or today's tree cannot hold. Never a blocker alone and no extra round: a named limit with a detection (a preflight query, or a refusal of the input).
  - **All classes.** A finding names its actor, what the actor needs and what it gains, or it is hardening. What is open after the last round is a named limit in the PR, acceptable only if: it cannot alone give a wrong auth, tenant, money or data outcome; reaching it needs a second failure; the PR and module contract record how it is noticed; the owner accepts the PR's limits in one answer. A HIGH design is approved once no structural blocker is left; local defects move to each slice's code review. A defect a review finds on `main` gets its own R-37 report and change; it does not grow the PR.
- **Skips.** Skip repeat full review by reviewing the change as a delta. (1) For a clean merge of `main`, Astra may limit the delta review to mechanical proof that the delta is exactly the new commits of `main`, touches none of the PR's files and had no conflict. Record that proof and the verdict on the new head. Required lanes and gate evidence stay under R-04. (2) PR wording edits that leave the head unchanged and make no security claim need no new head verdict. Changed commit messages and changed security claims get a delta review. (3) For a round the independent model refuses, a stand-in reviews that round's exact head at high effort or above: an isolated session of the other model family that did not write the change, is not the lead, and is named by the owner for that round or by a standing designation; the record names the refusal, the head, the stand-in and the owner's words. The next unrefused Astra round covers the full head. Rewording or retrying a refused prompt stays forbidden (R-25). A skip replaces only the Astra round: every other review the routing requires, such as Claude's review under R-01, still applies. The 2026-11-28 procedure preserves this refusal exception in full (owner, 2026-11-23: "Accept all"). Quota leaves review pending under R-25.
- **Cost of the decision, in numbers.** It stands in `RULES-REGISTERS.md`, under "The cost of R-02".
- **Checked by.** `ENFORCED` in part, proposed: CHK-01 checks for the required pre-push YES on each pushed head, with the stand-in record under skip (3) where applicable, delta evidence on later heads, and the additional HIGH review record where required.
- **Evidence.** Binding decision, CR-COMMON §2.1.

- **Gate flake exception** (owner, 2026-11-22: "yes, merge it now and make it a standing rule"; tightened 2026-11-25: "Approve all", after an agreed lead and Astra review). The Mac gate's `pr:verify` counts as passed without a rerun only when all of these hold:
  - **Complete Mac run on the exact head.** Setup passed. The E2E gate report is complete: exactly one unexpected failure, nothing interrupted or left unrun, no runner error.
    - The skipped cases match the expected skips by project, spec and full title. The expected set comes from the latest fully green Mac gate on `main` that covered the same projects, adjusted only for skips that the PR itself adds or removes and names. Naming a skip does not excuse any other skip.
    - Setup, gate and smoke each ran with zero retries and one worker, recorded for that invocation, and each kept its own JSON report.
    - A run stopped by the failure cap with cases unrun does not qualify.
  - **Mac smoke passed on that head, for both projects `ks-sq` and `mk-mk`.** When the gate's failure stopped `pr:verify` before smoke, a follow-up smoke run on the same head supplies it.
  - **Earlier stages passed.** Every earlier stage of the gate passed (contracts, RLS, tenant guard, build), and `security:guard` has a passed `GATE-EVIDENCE` stamp on that head.
  - **Same case on hosted CI.** Hosted PR E2E passed the same spec, full title and project on the same head. The only exception: a `gate-mk-mk` case may match its `gate-mk-contract` counterpart when that mapping is verified and recorded for that case. Every other case needs an exact project match.
    - All three hosted PR E2E lanes ran and passed on that head: `gate-ks-sq`, `gate-mk-contract` and smoke.
    - The case's first result on that head, across every workflow run and attempt, read from the matched lane's uploaded `report.json` (not a workflow summary), was a pass at retry 0.
    - No hosted lane or its setup shows a retry or a flaky result.
  - **Triage.** The failing code path lies outside the PR's change. From the Mac trace, the lead identifies the failure as a flake, names the failing operation, and rules out any contribution from the PR, including through shared dependencies. A known product defect is not a flake. When the evidence cannot tell a test race from a product defect, the merge waits.
  - **Budget.**
    - A logical test may be waived at most twice, counting every earlier exception merge since 2026-11-24. The ledger, kept in the lead's audit folder, supports a waiver only when it holds all nine exception merges of 2026-11-24 and 2026-11-25 (#2124, #2140, #2114, #2144, #2146, #2133, #2147, #2142, #2141) and every later one.
    - After its first waiver, a fix PR is open before the next gate starts.
    - A second waiver needs fresh evidence that the failure has the same cause as the first; a different cause needs a new diagnosis and doesn't qualify.
    - A third failure is not waived until the fix has merged.
    - Each waiver is recorded before the merge in the lead's flake ledger outside the repository, with its evidence. Without the ledger there is no waiver.
  - **Every other merge condition holds.**

  The exception stands in only for the passed `pr:verify` stamp of P3. That run's own stamp and record, with the one failure, are kept. The merge record contains or references retained evidence for every condition above: the failed test, its project and failure signature, the Mac setup, gate and smoke reports with their retry and worker settings, the skip comparison, the triage from the trace, each hosted lane's `report.json` across runs and attempts with the retry-0 result, the ledger entry and this rule. A reference to a ledger entry that retains these reports and comparisons satisfies this. ### R-08 — No failure is swallowed in server code **[class]**

### R-11 — Scheduled work is declared, and every send goes through a delivery record
- **Rule.** Before a scheduled job counts as delivered: (1) its schedule is declared in the repository, or the route is marked dormant in the same registry; (2) each item it fails on is recorded with its error, and a run in which an item failed is never answered as a success without that record; (3) a job that sends something — an e-mail, a push, a notice — sends only through a delivery record: the item is enqueued once under a unique key, claimed under a lease, and then finished as sent or skipped, or returned to the queue with its error and a later attempt time, and marked failed after a bounded number of attempts. A unique marker alone is not that: a marker written before the send loses the item when the run stops between the two, and a marker that nothing reads prevents nothing.
- **What this guarantees, and what it does not.** Delivery at least once: no item is lost silently, and a finished item is not sent again. A run that stops after the provider accepted the message and before the record is finished sends that one item a second time when the lease expires. Closing that gap needs an idempotency key that the sending provider honours, which is `UNVERIFIED` for the provider in use.
- **Checked by.** `ENFORCED` in part, proposed: **CHK-11**, two contract tests. *(a) Schedule registry:* every route under `apps/web/src/app/api/cron/` and every scheduled function appears in one registry as scheduled or dormant. It proves part (1) and nothing else. *(b) Delivery contract*, on a real database, for every sending job in the registry, four cases: an interruption after the claim and before the send is delivered once after the lease expires; a failed send is stored with its error code, comes back with a later attempt time, ends as failed at the attempt limit and then appears in the operator's list; a second run after a finished run sends nothing, and a second enqueue under the same key creates no second item; two workers that claim at the same time receive disjoint items. A sending job without these four cases cannot be registered as scheduled. Part (2), for a job that does not send, is checked for the shape of its `catch` by CHK-08 and is otherwise `ADVISORY`: whether a job reports a failed item truthfully is a review criterion.
- **Evidence.** MAIN-11 (S2, 2P): 5 of 6 cron routes have no scheduler in the repository. SCTX-02 (S2, 2P): the dunning job writes a marker and never reads it, so its reminders would repeat; registry membership alone would pass that job, which is why part (b) exists (review comment R2a-C09). SCTX-07 (S2, 2P): item failures lose their cause. What is sound, and is the model: the sponsor mail outbox has these states — queued, claimed under a ten-minute lease, sent, skipped, failed — with a claim that skips locked rows, a backoff, a limit of eight attempts and a unique idempotency key (`packages/database/src/schema/sponsor-roster.ts:121-168`; `packages/domain-membership-billing/src/sponsors/mail-outbox.ts:38-100`, read by the main session in R2b).

- **Checked by.** `ENFORCED` in part, exists: the pin (`package.json:289`, `packageManager`), the lockfile, and the ignore rules that keep the five directories out of every commit (`.gitignore:2,7,46,48,92`). CHK-02, proposed, fails an instruction file that prescribes `npm`. `ADVISORY` for a hand edit that stays local: it can make a check pass on a patched dependency or on a stale build, and no machine sees it.
- **Evidence.** Kept from `.github/copilot-instructions.md:5,8` at the pinned commit. The mapping had retired that span as "inferable from the code and enforced by the formatter, the linter or the compiler" without naming an enforcement (row OR-234; review comment R2b-C09); it is split in R3 and both halves are carried here. - **Changes.** `AGENTS.md`, one line.

### R-37 — Stop at a boundary: report, a review before the code goes further, the owner's authorization
- **Rule.** Before a change that touches or can alter one of six boundaries, and whenever a sign of a failure at one of them is met, inside the task or on the way to something else: stop, and report to the owner with file and line. The six boundaries: **authentication** (who a caller is: `packages/shared-auth`, `apps/web/src/lib/auth`, `apps/web/src/server/auth`); **sessions** (the claims of a session and its binding to a user and a tenant); **the database** (row policies, constraints, tenant filters, roles and grants: any change of `packages/database` that touches tenant or access-scoped behaviour); **tenancy** (how the tenant is resolved, and what a tenant, a branch or a role may see: `apps/web/src/lib/tenant`); **storage** (buckets, tenant paths, signed intents and the access-checked reads of documents); and **billing** (payments, entitlements and their events). "Can alter" includes a change of trust assumptions (which request data is trusted, where the tenant id comes from, impersonation), of role or tenant scope, of what is visible, of security behaviour such as validation, error boundaries or the handling of secrets, and of what the task is to achieve at the boundary, its acceptance. The work goes on only when two things have happened: the independent security reviewer — Astra, under R-02 — has reviewed the planned change or the suspected failure and has approved going on, **before the code goes further**, and the owner has explicitly authorized going on. A review alone is not enough: the reviewer's verdict says in terms that the work may go on, or what must change first, and a verdict with an open `blocker` is no approval (the old files asked for a security review, for an approval and for a sign-off: `packages/shared-auth/AGENTS.override.md:9,38`, `apps/web/src/server/auth/AGENTS.override.md:9`; added in the delta fix round, comment D01). The review of the final head (R-01, R-02) stays and does not replace this one. The PR holds the record of the stop: the first of the two lists below says, item by item, what it holds. Never weaken or bypass a check at a boundary: authentication, a role or permission check, a tenant filter, a row policy, a constraint, input validation, an error boundary (R-30 and R-35 say the same for identity and for the mandatory protections). A defect found this way is fixed inside the task only under the same authorization; otherwise it gets a change of its own at its own tier. Two lists follow, the record and the signs. Each binds as it is written, because `AGENTS.md`, R-37, names it.
  - **The record** (this list binds as written). The PR holds, in its list of exceptions and in its evidence section (`.github/pull_request_template.md`, "Pilot guardrails" and "Evidence"): (1) **the approval** — the security reviewer's approval of going on: who reviewed, the model that served, what was reviewed (the plan or the diff, with its commit) and the verdict; (2) **the authorization** — the owner's explicit authorization to go on; (3) **the exception** that was granted, with its security rationale; (4) **the scope** of the change at the boundary: its acceptance criteria, its risk, what the security review covered and, for a tenant change, the migrations it depends on; (5) **the tests**, added or adjusted with the change, and explicit about the behaviour at the boundary also where an addition breaks nothing — for authentication and roles: a test for every changed branch of an authentication or role path; the allowed path and the failure path of authentication and of the session; role-based access tests for each affected route and component; tests of authentication, of the role checks and of tenant scoping in every changed path of a server entry point; regression tests of the denied path and of the handling of failures — for the shared contracts: contract or interface tests of every session or authentication surface that the change touches; regression tests of role resolution and of tenant binding — for an API route: unit or integration tests of every changed contract; security regression checks of its authorization and validation paths — for tenancy and the database: tests of tenant-scoped reads, writes, queries and constraints; a test for every changed constraint, index, scope or tenant filter; explicit cross-tenant access tests of the denied and of the error paths; regression tests of cross-tenant isolation — and everywhere: a tenant, role or row-policy rule is tested on a real database under the restricted role (R-06); (6) **the evidence** of those tests and checks: the commands that were run, with their output; the criteria by which each counts as passed; the status of every check and guard that ran, passed or failed; and for a tenant change the branch coverage of the changed code and the failure signatures — what each denied and each failing path returned or logged, and which signs of the list below were looked for; (7) **for every change at the authentication or session boundary**: the proof that the change can be rolled back safely; and where it adjusts authentication or session behaviour, evidence that does not depend on a migration — it holds whether or not a migration has run ("migration-independent evidence"); (8) **for every database change that touches tenant-scoped or access-scoped behaviour, whether or not it changes the schema**: migration notes, with any step that an operator runs by hand and, for a destructive change, the migration strategy that was approved; and a rollback plan, with the evidence that the rollback is safe. Details and evidence of a new S0 or S1 finding stay in the restricted record outside the repository that the owner has authorized as part of the PR record (2026-11-18), with a reference in the PR that reveals nothing sensitive; every item of this list still exists before the work goes on or the change merges.
  - **The signs of a failure** (this list binds as written): an attempt to read or write a row of another tenant, or such a read or write; a tenant mismatch between a row and the session; a missing tenant predicate, tenant id or session where one is required; a tenant binding that is missing or ambiguous; a session claim that is missing, invalid or does not match; a session that fails validation or an integrity check, or a failed digest check; a session reused across tenants; a role resolved unexpectedly, a privilege that does not match the role, or a privilege that has drifted; a bypass of an authentication or authorization check, or the trace of one; a row policy that fails, is violated or filters inconsistently; a failed tenant constraint; a failed migration; a request validation failure that did not stop the request; server errors, token validation failures or unusual authentication or session behaviour at a boundary route or in its logs.
- **Checked by.** `ADVISORY`: no machine sees whether an agent stopped. What a machine and a reviewer see afterwards: CHK-01, proposed, refuses a HIGH change without an Astra review; the reviewer of R-01 reads the PR against the record's list, and a boundary change whose PR lacks the approval, the authorization or another item of that list is a `blocker`. No new check is proposed with this rule. **Cost, as far as it is known (CR-COMMON §2).** The rule adds no review that R-02 does not already give a HIGH change; it fixes when the first one happens, and it adds a stop and an authorization of the owner before that. How many changes of the 90-day window touched one of the six boundaries is `NOT MEASURED`.
- **Evidence.** It stands in `RULES-REGISTERS.md`, under "The evidence of R-37".
- **Failing example.** A change under `apps/web/src/lib/tenant/` that widens which members an agent's list shows, opened as a PR once it is written, with no report before it and no authorization in the PR. **Passing example.** The same change after a report to the owner, a review by Astra of the intended filter, with its approval, before more code is written, the owner's authorization, and a PR that holds the record: the approval, the authorization, the exception with its rationale, the tests of the denied and of the error paths, and their evidence with branch coverage and failure signatures.
- **Toward the destination.** Authentication boundary, tenant context, PostgreSQL with RLS: every layer that decides who may see what.
- **Changes.** `AGENTS.md`, one line. The six `AGENTS.override.md` files are removed in the adoption PR (`A/ADOPTION-PR-FILES.md`).

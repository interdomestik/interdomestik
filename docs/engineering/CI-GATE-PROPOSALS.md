**The three answers in short.**
1. **The path is one chain and one wait.** A PR takes a median of 34 minutes of checks because the browser job takes 30.5 minutes, and the required check `delivery-gate` waits for it and for twelve other checks. The second chain, `audit`, takes 19.5 minutes: removing the browser job alone would not bring a PR under 19 minutes.
2. **Every ready PR runs everything.** In the sample, a PR that changed `AGENTS.md` and one plan document took 25.9 minutes, and five PRs that changed only delivery tooling took 30 to 99 minutes. No heavy job was skipped on any of the 40 PRs.
3. **Target: 15 minutes at the median and 20 at the 90th percentile for an ordinary product PR, 5 minutes for a documentation or copy PR.** Four changes reach it, none of which removes a test: one build instead of two, the browser lanes side by side, the `audit` job split by what it guards, and lanes by risk tier. One required check that validates nothing is removed.

### 1.1 The measured time

`MEASURED` by the lead, arithmetic recomputed; n = 40 PRs, each on its final, green head. Failures, reruns and earlier heads are not in this sample (R1, A4 has those).

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| First check start to last check finish, per PR | median 34.3 min; 90th percentile 58.0; maximum 98.7; minimum 25.0 |
| Distribution | 0 PRs under 20 minutes; 11 in 20 to 30; 24 in 30 to 40; 5 above 40 (58.0, 60.3, 81.9, 90.2, 98.7) |

| Required | 9 status checks by branch protection, and one workflow by the organisation ruleset (the tenant guard). Six more checks are required only through the contract of `delivery-gate` |

What a PR pays in full is larger than one pass. In the 90-day window a PR had a median of 3 commits and 31 workflow runs, and 76.3% of PRs received commits after opening (R1, A4): each push starts the path again.

### 1.2 What determines it

```
push ─┬─ PR E2E Preflight 0.3 ── PR E2E Runner 30.5 ── e2e 0.1 ────────────┐
      ├─ validation-surface 0.3 ─┬─ audit 19.2                             │
      │                          ├─ unit 14.1                              ├─ delivery-gate finishes at 32.2
      │                          ├─ static 3.0                             │   (it polls; it runs no test)
      │                          └─ e2e-gate 2.5 (on a PR: the RLS test)   │
      ├─ Pilot Gate Preflight 0.3 ── Pilot Gate Runner 8.2 ── pilot-gate 0.1
      └─ twenty short checks, each under 5 minutes ────────────────────────┘
```

- **The longest chain** is `PR E2E Preflight` → `PR E2E Runner` → `e2e`: 30.9 minutes by the medians (`.github/workflows/e2e-pr.yml:14-151`). The runner is one job on one machine. It installs, migrates a fresh database, seeds it, builds the application and runs two Playwright lanes one after the other, each with one worker (`e2e-pr.yml:90-116`; `scripts/run-e2e-lane.mjs:41-46,69`; `scripts/m4-gatekeeper.sh:237-245`).
- **`delivery-gate` adds about 1.3 minutes of its own** (`INFERRED`: 32.2 against 30.9). It polls every 30 seconds and, after a green result, waits 20 seconds and reads everything again (`scripts/ci/pr-delivery-api.mjs:5`; `scripts/ci/pr-delivery-contract.json`, `quiescenceMs`; `scripts/ci/pr-delivery-gate.mjs:209-233`).
- **The second chain** is `validation-surface` → `audit`: 19.5 minutes. `audit` is one job with 17 steps (`.github/workflows/ci.yml:45-89`). Its largest step is `INFERRED` to be the tests of the CI tooling itself: 282 test files under `scripts/ci/`, in four shards on one machine (`ci.yml:79`; `scripts/ci/contract-test-shards.mjs:10-55`).
- **The third** is `unit`: 14.4 minutes, the coverage run on one machine, with the build cache switched off for this job (`ci.yml:113-144`, lines 121-124).

`NOT MEASURED`: the duration of each step inside a job, the time a job waits for a machine, and how far the jobs overlap. The export holds one duration per check. The job records of the same 40 heads hold the step times; reading them is the first action of part 3.

## 2. Every check: keep, change, merge, move or remove

How to read the table. **Caught** is what R1 could show (A5): a failure on one head followed by a success on a later head, with the failed step and the next commit read on a sample. An empty sample is not proof of no protection (CR-COMMON §4.6). **Runs** are those of the check's workflow in the 90 days. A proposal that removes or merges a check states the A5 condition it meets: (i) execution count, complete evidence, demonstrated overlap and replacement coverage; or (ii) the guarded obligation and all its consumers are retired. A proposal that only changes how a check runs removes nothing; rule R-10 applies to it (a trial on merged changes before it is required).

### 3.3 What the proposals do not do

- They remove no test and lower no threshold.
- They do not touch the tenant guard or its ruleset.
- They do not change what `delivery-gate` waits for, until the review record exists.
- They do not rely on a re-run: R-14 forbids weakening a test to pass a gate, and a retried result does not count as green (R-04).

## 4. The proposed checks, one by one

The 26 checks of `RULES.md` ride on five carriers: the existing lint job, the existing contract-test shard, one change-policy step, the CI workflow and the instruction files. They add two CI steps and one job, and no new required workflow.

### 4.1 The rule for thresholds: start at today's count, and only fall

1. **A baseline has a unit and a predicate.** A number without both is not a baseline (review comment R2a-C10). Each count below was re-measured at the pinned commit by a script that states both (`A/raw/R2b/gates/chk-baselines.json`, unit U-CHK on Opus 5.5; scripts under `A/raw/R2b/gates/chk/`; the main session re-measured CHK-04, CHK-08 and CHK-13 itself and re-read the failing examples it cites).
2. **The baseline is taken by the check itself, on the adoption commit**, and stored as a list of items, not as a number. Today's counts are what to expect, not the stored baseline.
3. **The ratchet.** The check fails on an item that is not on the list (a new violation), and on a listed item that no longer violates (the entry is removed in the same PR). The list can only shrink. A PR that adds an entry is a T6 change that the owner approves, and CHK-03 flags it.
4. **A check that starts at zero has no list**: CHK-02, CHK-09 for rebuilt screens, CHK-12, CHK-13 after WP-04, CHK-15 part (a), CHK-23 after WP-01, CHK-25.
5. **Trial before requirement (R-10).** Every check runs as a report on 20 merged PRs before it can fail a PR; false positives found in the trial are fixed in the predicate, not by an exception.
6. **Counting the effect** is in `EFFECT-CHECK.md`, §4: executions, catches and escapes per check and 30 days.

**A baseline entry is not a pass** (added after the replay of R3). In the replay no defect was caught by a check, and in three cases the nearest check would have let the defect through, once because its baseline held the code. A check with a baseline therefore also reports: when a change touches a file that stands on a baseline, the PR is told which entries the file carries, and the review record of R-01 says whether they were fixed. It blocks nothing; it makes the list fall where work is done anyway.

### 4.3 The instruction-drift guard, with its failing and its passing example

**The list of identifiers** is one file in the repository, in two parts (proposed content: `A/raw/R3/corrections/drift-identifiers.txt`; corrected in R3, review comment R2b-C01). In the repository it is a JSON file directly under `scripts/ci/`, not a `.txt` file: a guard that runs today refuses a changed text file that it cannot classify, and a structured file without a registered owner (part 5). *Prohibited now*, four patterns: the `supabase` command and its `npx` form; `db push` and `drizzle-kit push` (the guarded `db:push:local` stays); `npm install`, `npm run`, `npm ci`; and, for a skill, a description that starts the finished program on a project name or an ordinary word. *Conditional*, five patterns: `pnpm legacy:validate`, `pnpm plan:audit:legacy`, `pnpm test:harness-v2`, the `slice:*` entry points and the slice-runner script files. **The conditional ones still exist in the repository.** The required check `audit` still runs the legacy validation; the slice runners are retired by your decision of 2026-11-17 (question 32, condition (ii)), and their removal is a PR of its own after the adoption (part 6.2). While a conditional identifier has a consumer, the guard fails it only in a trigger position — a text that starts it without being asked — and reports, without failing, one that stands in a command block or a required step. An identifier moves to the first part in the same PR that removes its last consumer: the slice entry points and the runner scripts with the PR that removes the runners, the legacy commands with the approved change of `audit`. The guard reads two positions only: a fenced command block, and the text under a heading or a field that triggers or requires (a skill's `description`, "Use this skill whenever", "Run", "Required"). It does not promise to detect contradictory prose.

**This part is the one specification of the guard** (closing pass, review comment R3-C06). **The file-size guard is a typed guard, and wider than its row above** (corrected in R3; read at `scripts/lib/modularity-guard.mjs:81-91,150-156,170-248` and `scripts/modularity-guard-policy.mjs:1-15,44-75,270-294`; this document had described a limit on production files only). For every text file that a PR changes or adds it applies the rule of the file's class. Production code: as in the row. A test file: at most 300 lines. A Markdown file, except five exempt paths of which `AGENTS.md` is one: at most 1,000 lines and 131,072 bytes; a changed one must keep **every** heading of its base version; a new one must have a heading. A changed `.json`, `.jsonl`, `.toml`, `.yaml` or `.yml` file: a registered owner (`:44-75`), at most 131,072 bytes, and for JSON the formatter's canonical form. A text file of no class, a `.txt` file for example: refused. A deleted file is not looked at, so a deletion passes where a shortening fails. Three consequences. (1) Every registry or list that a check of part 4 reads is a JSON file directly under `scripts/ci/`, whose files are registered as script configuration (`:58`); the same file under `packages/database/` would be refused (`A/adoption-pr/CHECKS.json`, the controls). (2) An instruction file cannot become a short pointer unless the old headings stay, and a document above 131,072 bytes can be neither added nor edited: the adoption PR is built for both (`A/ADOPTION-PR-FILES.md`). (3) A document that stands just under the budget cannot grow, which is why the adoption splits the three largest by part.

**Three proposals, none of which removes a guard.**
1. **One step per guard** in the workflow, or one line per guard in the job summary. Today one step runs all ten after the lockfile scan, and the scan stops the run on a hit, so R1 could not attribute a single catch to a guard: the five sampled failures of this step are read as banned dependency versions (`A/R1-evidence.md`, A5).
2. **CHK-10 applies to them.** No guard's test names all four negative inputs; one names three. Whether a guard fails closed on an unparseable input is `NOT MEASURED`.
3. **The allow-list of raw role arrays ratchets** like the lists of part 4.1: 40 entries today, and the number only falls.

The per-file limit is 300 lines, not 150: the number an older plan calls "150-line modularity, already CI-enforced" is the advisory threshold (`scripts/modularity-guard-policy.mjs:2,8`). The repository-size check is a different guard, in `audit` and before every push: its budget file was edited by 124 commits in August and September and by none since, and no refusal for another reason than its own budget was found (`A/raw/R2b/gates/gates.md`, §5).
